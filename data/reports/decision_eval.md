## What is evaluated

The decisions the agent takes on every turn, before it converses: **D1** intent (5 classes), **D2** does the customer want a person?, **D3** is theft suspected? The policy (`policy.yaml`) acts on their probabilities through thresholds.

| Candidate | What it is |
|---|---|
| Baseline | ES/PT keyword rules (`decision_layer.keywords`) |
| LLM | The agent's model with structured output (`llm:openai/gpt-6-luna`), no fallback |
| Jev | TypeSafe AI through OpenRouter (`llm:openrouter/typesafe/jev-router`), with the same structured-output adapter as the LLM, no fallback |

## Data, labels and leakage

- **320 messages** from 80 seeds (4 paraphrases each): 216 in Spanish (MX/CO/AR), 80 in Portuguese (BR), 24 in portuñol (mixed). It includes negations ("no quiero hablar con nadie"), figurative theft ("me están robando con la comisión") and slang.
- **Provenance:** synthetic text written by the team for this evaluation (`data/eval/decisions/seeds.yaml`); it is not customer data and does not come from the dataset, whose texts are templates (`insights.md` §5).
- **Labels:** one annotator, following the definitions in `decision_layer/questions.py`. **Blind second annotation pending:** `data/eval/decisions/second_labeling.csv` (40 messages) for another person to label without seeing the labels; this report computes the agreement (kappa) once it is done.
- **Split by seed, no leakage:** all paraphrases of a seed stay on the same side; stratified by intent. **Validation** (160) is used only to choose thresholds; **test** (160) is reported once.

## What the results say

1. **Baseline:** gets the intent right 44.4% of the time [30-60]; at the policy threshold it decides 31.2% of the messages and asks for clarification on the rest. Across languages: no significant difference.
2. **LLM:** 97.5% average accuracy [86-99], at US$0.122 per 1,000 messages and ~2.1 s latency (p50); at the policy threshold it decides 100.0% and is right 96.9% of the time. Across languages: no significant difference.
3. **Recommendation:** use `DECISIONS=llm` (with the baseline as the fallback if the model fails): its accuracy beats the baseline with intervals that do not overlap. The latency adds to every turn, because the policy routes the turn with these decisions before the model converses.
4. **Policy thresholds:** wants a person 0.50 inside the valid range; theft 0.30 inside the valid range; intent 0.50 is right on 96.9% of what it decides. With the LLM, the current thresholds are backed by this evaluation.
5. **Jev:** 97.1% average accuracy [88-100], ~3.1 s (p50); at the policy threshold it decides 100.0% and is right 98.1% of the time. Against the LLM: the intervals overlap, so with this data they are equivalent. Policy thresholds with Jev: pide persona inside; robo inside.

## Limitations

- **The D3 guideline allows two readings:** it includes "use by third parties", and a phrase like "una compra que yo no hice" can be read that way. When reviewing the theft errors listed above, the second annotation (column `theft`) will tell whether they are model errors or label errors.
- One annotator until the second annotation is done; the labels may carry the author's bias.
- Synthetic text: it does not replace real customer messages; portuñol and slang are an approximation.
- Small n for D3 (theft) and portuñol: the intervals are wide.
- Jev is evaluated through OpenRouter, not Cloudflare Workers AI; its cost does not show because LiteLLM has no price for it.
- The LLM's probabilities are the ones the model states; the ECE measures how far they can be trusted.
